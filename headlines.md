# Headline Engine

## Governing standard

A headline must stand on its own, convey the piece fairly, include indispensable context, match the piece's tone, and invite attention without misleading or overpromising. Distinguish comment, conjecture, prediction, and fact. Optimize for the right reader's informed interest, not raw clicks.

First identify the artifact: working title, pitch subject line, editor-facing headline, published headline, or deck. Apply its venue-specific job and constraints.

## Generate a broad slate

Draft enough candidates to create real strategic range; 8–12 is useful for a full headline pass, not a quota for every task. Use several families:

1. **Direct thesis:** names the claim cleanly.
2. **Consequence/stakes:** foregrounds what changes or what is at risk.
3. **Reframe:** replaces the audience's default frame with the piece's central insight.
4. **Tension/paradox:** names two truths in productive conflict.
5. **Specific image or object:** uses a concrete element that genuinely carries the argument.
6. **Action/actor:** names who should do what, when the piece is prescriptive.
7. **Explanatory:** “Why/How X...” only when the article actually answers it.
8. **Voice-led:** uses restrained wit, contrast, or cadence native to the author.

Avoid creating twelve syntactic variants of one idea.

## Score candidates

Score 0–2 on each dimension:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fidelity | Distorts or exceeds piece | Mostly accurate | Exact promise/content match |
| Specificity | Generic | Some concrete signal | Distinctive and appropriately concrete |
| Clarity | Hard to parse | Understandable | Immediate and clean |
| Interest | Flat or familiar | Some tension | Earned curiosity or consequence |
| Stakes | No reason to care | Implied | Clear, proportional consequence |
| Voice/tone | Mismatched | Neutral | Fits author and venue |
| Freshness | Cliché/template | Familiar construction | Non-gimmicky and memorable |
| Search/discovery | Omits essential subject | Partial | Names useful entities/topics naturally |

Fidelity, indispensable context, factual implication, and legal/reputational risk are veto dimensions. Never recommend a candidate that fails one, regardless of total.

## Run the promise audit

For each finalist, answer:

- What does this headline explicitly claim?
- What does it imply?
- Where does the piece pay off each promise?
- What essential qualifier or context is lost?
- Would a skeptical subject say it misrepresents the article?
- Does it blur comment, conjecture, prediction, or fact?
- Does omitted context create a materially different impression?

## Use engagement research carefully

Large-scale headline research suggests reader interest is not maximized by maximal vagueness or maximal specificity. Aim for enough concrete information to establish subject and stakes while leaving a legitimate question the article resolves. This is a heuristic, not a license for curiosity gaps.

Negative headline language increased click-through in a large randomized Upworthy dataset, but engagement is not a quality, accuracy, or trust standard. Use negative, emotional, or sensational wording only when proportionate and independently justified. Never add urgency, certainty, conflict, novelty, or causality the article does not establish.

## Reject these patterns

- the headline's strongest claim appears nowhere in the article;
- question headlines that merely insinuate;
- “Everything you need to know,” “game-changing,” “shocking,” or similar inflation;
- abstract nouns without actor, action, tension, or consequence;
- unexplained acronyms or insider language;
- cleverness that delays comprehension;
- claims about “what everyone gets wrong” without evidence;
- headline/deck repetition.

## Deliver recommendations

Return the slate grouped by family, then recommend:

- **Best editorial headline:** strongest fidelity + argument signal.
- **Best broad-audience headline:** clearest accessible stakes.
- **Best voice-led headline:** most distinctive without gimmickry.

Include a one-sentence rationale and flag venue-dependent length or style constraints.
