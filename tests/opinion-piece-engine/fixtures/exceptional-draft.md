# The Small Mercy of a Stop Rule

Institutions like beginnings. We cut ribbons, announce pilots and photograph the first day. Endings receive less ceremony, especially when the thing ending once appeared in a strategic plan.

That asymmetry matters for educational technology. A pilot without a stop rule is not quite a pilot; it is a purchase wearing temporary clothes. The distinction is not whether leaders feel optimistic. It is whether they have named, in advance, the evidence that would justify continuing—and the evidence that would require them to stop.

This sounds procedural until a tool settles into a teacher's week. Accounts are created. Lessons bend around its workflow. Someone learns to produce the report that someone else learns to request. None of this proves the tool is bad. It explains why reversibility must be designed before attachment makes reversal expensive.

The humane promise of a stop rule is not that institutions will avoid mistakes. It is that people will not have to pretend every mistake was a strategy.
