# Revision and Evaluation

## Diagnose before rewriting

Name the dominant problem in one sentence. Classify it:

- assignment/audience;
- thesis or stakes;
- argument/evidence;
- structure/pacing;
- first-screen bridge;
- opening/ending;
- paragraph logic/transitions;
- voice/style;
- headline/venue fit;
- verification/integrity.

Prioritize at most three changes per pass. Explain each with evidence from the draft.

## Analytic rubric

Score each criterion 1–4:

| Criterion | 1 — Failing | 2 — Emerging | 3 — Strong | 4 — Exceptional |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thesis | Topic, truism, bundle, or unsupported | Contestable but broad/blurred | Clear, scoped, supportable | Distinctive insight that organizes every move |
| Stakes/audience | No “why care” or reader model | Stakes asserted; audience generic | Consequence and persuadable audience clear | Stakes reshape how the audience sees the issue |
| Reasoning | Assertions or gaps | Some warrants; notable leaps | Claims, warrants, qualifications cohere | Complex reasoning feels inevitable and lucid |
| Evidence | Thin, irrelevant, invented, or overstated | Mixed quality or loose claim fit | Credible, proportional, well-integrated | Evidence changes the reader's understanding and survives scrutiny |
| Fairness | Straw man or omission of decisive complication | Acknowledges resistance superficially | Engages relevant objection/limit honestly | Opposition materially strengthens or refines the thesis |
| Structure/pacing | Accumulation without motion | Recognizable sequence with drag | Purposeful escalation and variation | Form produces discovery and argumentative momentum |
| Opening | Generic, deceptive, or disconnected | Relevant but familiar/slow | Specific, engaging, promise aligned | Creates the exact question the piece is built to answer |
| Ending | Summary, slogan, or new topic | Competent but predictable | Lands implication or reframes thesis | Final turn enlarges meaning without overclaiming |
| Voice | Generic, imitative, or inflated | Intermittently distinctive | Consistent, credible, author-owned | Voice and reasoning are inseparable; memorable without performance |
| Prose/transitions | Opaque, padded, monotonous | Understandable with friction | Clear, varied, economical | Precision, rhythm, and movement reinforce meaning |
| Headline | Misleading, generic, or gimmicky | Accurate but weak | Accurate, specific, interesting | Compresses argument/stakes with distinctive fidelity |
| Integrity/fit | Unverified claims or venue mismatch | Gaps remain visible | Claims verified; constraints met | Transparent, meticulous, publication-ready |

Do not reduce the piece to a total score alone. Treat any score of 1 in thesis, evidence, headline fidelity, or integrity as a release blocker. A score of 4 should be rare and justified with observable features.

## Revision passes

### Pass 1: controlling idea

- Write the actual thesis in one sentence.
- Write the thesis the draft appears to argue.
- Resolve the mismatch.
- Cut or relocate material serving another essay.

### Pass 2: argument integrity

- Trace every reason to the thesis.
- Add missing warrants.
- Narrow claims that exceed evidence.
- Engage only relevant objections and limits.

### Pass 3: architecture and pace

- Label every paragraph's dominant job.
- Reorder by logic, discovery, or chronology intentionally.
- Compress repeated setup and evidence piles.
- Create pace changes at moments of consequence.
- Map reader state after each section: knows, feels, doubts, expects.
- Vary scene/summary, abstraction/concreteness, evidence density, sentence compression, and paragraph expansion intentionally.
- For each major bridge, name the relationship: inference, contrast, concession, consequence, scale shift, chronology, example, or return.
- Ask why the next section follows; do not repair missing logic with a connective word.

### Pass 4: opening and ending

- Test whether the opening's promise is paid off.
- Ensure the ending delivers a final turn, implication, image, judgment, or specific action rather than recap.
- Avoid introducing a new load-bearing claim in the ending.
- Choose an ending function: implication, changed callback, narrowed judgment, feasible action, projection, unresolved moral pressure, or scale return.
- Reject false uplift, slogans, grandiose universalization, decorative callbacks, and abrupt calls to action.

### Pass 5: voice and sentences

- Replace generic abstractions with precise nouns and verbs.
- Remove prefatory hedging that does not reflect real uncertainty.
- Retain qualifications required by evidence.
- Vary rhythm; cut sloganized parallelism used merely for effect.
- Remove “AI voice” markers: empty scene-setting, canned pivots, inflated triads, repetitive conclusions, and synthetic quotations.

### Pass 6: release check

- Verify factual claims, quotations, links, names, dates, numbers, and permissions.
- Confirm word count, style, disclosure, exclusivity, and submission requirements for the current venue.
- Run the headline promise audit.
- List unresolved editorial queries.
- Assign a release status; do not infer publication readiness from the score alone.

## Comparative testing

When choosing among versions, compare them blind where possible on:

- thesis recall after one read;
- perceived credibility;
- strongest remembered evidence;
- clarity of stakes;
- where attention falls;
- whether the headline's promise was paid;
- whether the voice sounds like the author.

Do not optimize only for preference or click likelihood.
