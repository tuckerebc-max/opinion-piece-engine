# Venue, Audience, and Voice

## Venue strategy

For a named venue, verify current requirements from the venue itself. Then analyze 6–12 recent comparable pieces when the decision merits the work. Compare form, audience, topic function, author standing, length, opening speed, thesis explicitness, evidence density, tone, headlines/decks, objection handling, and endings.

Produce:

| Dimension | Venue pattern | This piece | Conform or depart? | Rationale/risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Distinguish recurring patterns from single-piece flourishes. Do not treat frequency as a rule or imitate signature prose.

Map four audiences:

1. commissioning editor or gatekeeper;
2. persuadable primary reader;
3. people represented or affected by the argument;
4. secondary audiences whose reaction changes the consequences.

If no venue is known, build a portable core and identify decisions that must wait. If browsing is unavailable, label requirements and corpus patterns unverified.

## Author standing

Identify why this author can make this argument: expertise, lived experience, firsthand observation, reporting access, institutional responsibility, or a well-supported synthesis. Do not confuse title with standing or hide conflicts of interest.

State the standing claim the piece will actually make and the limits it will respect.

## Audience model

For each important audience, record:

| Current belief/resistance | Why reasonable or durable | Values/experience | What could move it | What will backfire |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use audience values to generate hypotheses, never stereotypes. More facts alone may not move a reader whose disagreement concerns trust, identity, incentives, or values.

## Voice brief

Build only from authentic author samples:

- stance toward the reader;
- syntax, cadence, and paragraph rhythm;
- abstraction versus concreteness;
- humor and emotional temperature;
- moral vocabulary and degree of certainty;
- favored evidence, analogy, and example patterns;
- first-person authority and epistemic modesty;
- phrases or habits to preserve;
- language the author would never use;
- top three preservation priorities.

After substantial revision, compare representative before/after passages. Explain any deliberate departure. Do not flatten every writer into short, declarative “op-ed voice.”

Research basis: https://doi.org/10.17226/23674 and https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167215607842. Apply audience-reframing evidence cautiously; much of it comes from polarized U.S. political contexts.
