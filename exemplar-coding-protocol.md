# Exemplar coding protocol

Use this protocol to study craft without copying prose or mistaking prevalence for quality.

## Sample design

Code 6–12 legally accessible pieces per research cycle. Deliberately vary venue, ideology, subject, author standing, and form: argument-led op-ed, narrative-led guest essay, reported column, expert intervention, first-person essay, cultural criticism, and policy argument. Include successful exceptions that omit anecdotes, explicit counterarguments, current pegs, or calls to action.

Record title, author, venue, publication date, stable locator, form, topic, author standing, apparent audience, and selection rationale. Do not store full copyrighted text. Retain only brief compliant excerpts when necessary and otherwise paraphrase structural observations.

## Coding fields

- Thesis location, explicitness, scope, claim type, and qualification.
- First-screen sequence: hook, context, standing, pivot, public question, thesis or controlled promise.
- Claim–evidence–warrant links; evidence type, density, and provenance.
- Anecdote function and the evidence that permits any generalization.
- Objections, rival explanations, trade-offs, concessions, and thesis changes.
- Stakes ladder and whether urgency is evidenced or inflated.
- Reader-state changes, paragraph jobs, transition functions, and pace shifts.
- Voice: reader stance, cadence, abstraction, humor, temperature, moral vocabulary, certainty.
- Ending type and whether its final inference was earned.
- Headline/deck promise, specificity, tone, and fidelity to the body.
- Meaningful omissions and venue-specific conventions.

## Synthesis discipline

Have two coders independently assess a subset where feasible. Record disagreements and revise ambiguous field definitions. Report recurring moves, meaningful exceptions, and failure patterns separately. Treat coded frequency as descriptive, not causal. Before converting an observation into engine guidance, triangulate it with editorial standards, writing scholarship, empirical research, or explicit practitioner judgment.
